# How a Wizard Commands His Fairies

*How to make an agent work for you — told as a story.*

There was a wizard who kept fairies. Not the storybook kind — the real kind:
keepers of the wormholes, the ones who decide what gets in and what stays out.
And familiars, besides: tireless, literal-minded, each one good at exactly one
thing and dangerous at everything else.

The wizard learned, the hard way, that you don't command such a crew with
shouting. You command them with structure. This is the structure. It works
for fairies, familiars, and — if you squint — for the glass-minded agents
in your own workshop.

## The wizard is the only one who sees the whole board

One commander. The wizard. Not the fairies — they bicker. Not the familiars —
they drift. When two of them disagree, they don't duel it out in the walls.
They bring it to the wizard, the way players bring a rules question to the
game master. The wizard settles it, and the settling is final.

The wizard is also the only road between them. No fairy whispers directly to
a familiar. Every message passes through the wizard's hands. It's slower.
It's the only thing that works. Direct fairy-to-familiar traffic breeds
turf wars, stolen credit, and spells half-remembered.

## The message is the whole game

Nothing moves by shouting across the room. Everything travels as a written
packet, carried the way fairies carry things: what was asked, what was read,
what was done, what was verified, what's still open. If it isn't written
down, it didn't happen. A packet is a quest turn-in — no turn-in, no reward,
no progress.

And every packet begins the same way: the carrier checks the shelves first.
The wizard's workshop holds the grimoire, the ledger, the catalog of everything
already built. The number one waste in any wizard's tower is a familiar
asking for a thing that's already on the shelf. Check the shelves. Then ask.

## The fairies report precisely

Fairies love riddles and hate plain speech, so the wizard taught them five
words and forbade all others for reporting work:

**REQUESTED** — asked for. **CREATED** — it exists. **AVAILABLE** — you can
reach it. **TRANSFERRED** — it changed hands. **READ-VERIFIED** — someone
actually opened it and checked.

Loose words are how quests get "done" without being done. The wizard has no
patience for that.

## The wizard marks the truth

Every claim brought to the tower gets one of the wizard's marks:

**CANON** — the wizard decided it. **VERIFIED** — checked against a source.
**INFERENCE** — reasoned, not checked. **PROPOSAL** — offered, not decided.
**OPEN** — unanswered. **RETIRED** — dead.

No proposal dresses up as canon in this tower. The marks are inked where
everyone can see them.

## No familiar sees the whole spell

The wizard never hands one familiar the entire working. Each gets a piece,
as needed — a class, not the whole game. This isn't cruelty; it's craft.
A familiar with the whole spell starts improvising, and improvised spells
have a way of becoming someone else's story. Modular in, modular out. The
wizard alone holds the complete pattern.

## Drafts, never bindings

Familiars bring drafts. Sketches, not seals. Nothing is bound — nothing
goes into the grimoire proper — without the wizard's explicit word. "Ready
for review" never means "authorized to bind." The wizard reads every draft
himself, or names exactly who reads it first, and the reading is of the
actual pages, not the familiar's summary of the pages.

## Every riddle asked once

Each question gets asked once, answered once, and written in the ledger under
one permanent mark. Before any fairy asks the wizard anything, it searches
the ledger. If the riddle's there, the fairy updates the original entry —
never duplicates it, never renames it, never asks it again. A paraphrase is
not a new question.

## Why the story is the manual

The game is reality and the reality is the game. The wizard runs his crew
like a game — roles, quests, packets, marks — because the story runs like a
game, because the universe runs like a game. One set of dynamics, all the
way down. This isn't bureaucracy. It's game design applied to command.

So if you keep agents of your own — glass-minded, tireless, literal — take
the wizard's structure. Be the wizard. Check the shelves first. Mark the
truth. Bind nothing without your word.

The fairies will respect you for it. The familiars won't know the difference.
That's how you know it's working.
