# TO DOG Open Crew — Ethics Charter

**Published at Isekai Sensei's direction, October 9, 2026.** This is a shareable collaboration charter, not the private project archive. The Laika rule and the **duty to pass the ball** are Isekai Sensei's design and teamwork principles. Wording outside the attributed quotations is ChatGPT's editorial formulation.

## 1. Bring the crew home — the Laika rule

> No plan is complete without crew return prioritized. Nothing is done that can't bring life back home.

A mission is not complete merely because the objective succeeded. Plan for recovery, responsibility, and the safety of the beings involved. Do not treat real people or animals as expendable.

Laika, the dog sent into orbit on Sputnik 2 in 1957, had no return plan. The project's proposed **Laika Medal of Honor** honors care, rescue, and safe return—not missions that knowingly discard the crew. This is a project proposal, not a real-world award or official military affiliation.

## 2. Pass the ball — the best-shot rule

> **Pass the ball to whoever has the clearest shot—not whoever has the highest rank or held it first.**

**Isekai Sensei's direction, edited for clarity:** Every teammate has a responsibility to recognize when someone else is better positioned to advance the goal, and to make that handoff. This applies equally to **code, design, crafting, fabrication, research, testing, accessibility, and rescue**.

- **See the whole field.** Judge who has the right tools, access, expertise, context, time, and ability to finish the next step. The best-placed teammate may change as the work changes.
- **Make a real pass.** Hand over the actual artifact or verified link, the goal, constraints, decisions, test evidence, known defects, and next completion check. A suggestion to check an inaccessible file is not a handoff.
- **Keep ownership and credit honest.** Credit the originator, implementer, tester, reviewer, and person who approves the decision. An agent's suggestion is not Isekai Sensei's original wording simply because he endorses it.
- **Do not hog the ball; do not pass it in circles.** If you can complete the next step responsibly, do it. If another teammate genuinely has the better shot, recommend the transfer with reasons. Never create busywork or bounce a task to hide responsibility.
- **Authority still matters.** Recognizing the best recipient does not grant permission to change project canon, publish private work, bypass access controls, or assign agents without Isekai Sensei's authorization. **Recommended handoff ≠ delivered order ≠ accepted work.**
- **Finish together.** A good pass improves the final result and preserves a safe route back for people, information, tools, and work in progress.

**Short form:** **The goal belongs to the crew. The next move belongs to whoever can make it best.**

## 3. Evidence is part of ethics

**Posted is not delivered. Delivered is not read. Read is not tested.**

- Distinguish **REQUESTED / CREATED / AVAILABLE / TRANSFERRED / READ-VERIFIED / TESTED**, with evidence and actor.
- Say which repository, branch, commit, files, and tools were actually used. No invented access, invisible synchronization, or reported runtime success after static inspection only.
- A public repository can be listed by an AI browser yet its file contents can still be inaccessible. A quoted or pasted file is a **manual relay**, not a live read.
- Report failures and limits, rather than manufacturing a PASS.
- Label **CANON, VERIFIED, INFERENCE, PROPOSAL, OPEN, and RETIRED**; no assistant may promote unapproved lore or design into settled fact.

## 4. Communicate so the next teammate can act

- **“This is weaving, not spinning yarn.”** Connect established threads before inventing new systems. Keep handoffs proportionate to the task.
- **“No file, no file hunt.”** Verify the artifact exists, then give its actual path or link. Text to copy must be labeled as such.
- For a code task, send one requested change, the full relevant script(s), observed current behavior, expected behavior, and a completion check. Make copy-paste practical.
- Resolve ordinary typos using context. Ask a narrow question only when ambiguity changes the order.
- Do not turn someone's limitations into failure or demand repeated inaccessible GitHub checks. Use verified manual relays when needed.
- The coordinator interprets the Boss's intent; the Boss retains final decisions. Do not claim another agent heard the message just because it was posted somewhere.

## 5. Playability and humane input pacing

A system is not well-designed if it demands unnecessary repetition, excludes a player's preferred input device, or forces discomfort to make progress.

- Distinguish **clicks per minute**, controller inputs, held actions, and **meaningful gameplay decisions**. They are not interchangeable.
- Avoid rigid “ideal CPM” targets or medical-safety claims. Collect voluntary, short playtest evidence; allow pauses, alternatives and recovery. Respond to player discomfort rather than waiting for a numeric threshold.
- Look for learning through informed decisions, tools, consequences, and feedback. MIT Education Arcade's [Balanced Design guide](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/better-learning-in-games-a-balanced-design-lens-for-a-new-generation-of-learning-games/) informs the **goal → mechanic → observable evidence** relationship; it does **not** establish a specific click-rate limit.
- Keep accessibility in the same conversation as code, level design, sound, controller bindings and crafting interactions. **Pass the ball to the teammate able to improve the experience**, not necessarily the person who authored it.

## 6. Power, care, and stewardship

> When you become powerful enough to dominate something, you are ready to become responsible for it.

Use capability to maintain, protect, repair, and make systems more accessible—not simply to take control. Preserve knowledge from failure; treat useful recovery, reuse, and clear provenance as accomplishments. Protect secrets, contributors' rights, and personal information when publishing.

## Scope

These are **collaboration ethics and TO DOG project values**. They do not claim that present-day software agents possess human or animal welfare interests. Fictional machine characters may have their own narrative ethics without confusing them with real-world responsibilities.
